# 18 — Company Structure and Investment Readiness

**Product:** Travelar  
**Parent company specified by the owner:** OuterBlocks Limited  
**Intended operating subsidiary specified by the owner:** OuterBlocks Destinations Limited  
**Status:** Preliminary corporate strategy; legal, tax and company-record verification required  
**Updated:** 23 October 2026

## 1. Purpose and confirmed position

The product name is **Travelar**. The product owner has confirmed the intended corporate structure: **OuterBlocks Limited** is the parent company and **OuterBlocks Destinations Limited** is the operating subsidiary/entity of record. The earlier draft name **Travelar Destinations Limited** is superseded. The actual incorporation, legal names, current directors/shareholders, parent relationship, corporate objects, tax registrations, licenses, bank/PSP accounts, and intellectual-property/intercompany agreements still require review of records; the owner decision is not itself registry evidence.

The practical recommendation is to begin with one correctly registered and appropriately authorized operating entity for the approved Tanzania activities, unless qualified Tanzanian counsel and tax/accounting advisers identify a specific reason that another structure is required or materially advantageous. A holding company or separate technology/travel subsidiary should be a later, evidence-driven decision—not an assumed prerequisite to product development.

## 2. Structure options

| Model | Benefits | Costs/risks | When to consider |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent + operating subsidiary (OuterBlocks Limited + OuterBlocks Destinations Limited) | Separates group ownership/technology interests from travel-marketplace contracting and operations as intended by the owner | Requires verified ownership, intercompany/IP terms, tax, transfer pricing, accounting, employment and data-role analysis; adds administrative cost | Intended direction confirmed by product owner; implement only after corporate and legal evidence review |
| Parent/holding company plus operating subsidiary | Can centralize group ownership and investment; can separate some activities contractually; future equity can be issued at an appropriate level | More company filings, accounting, tax returns, board governance, banking, intercompany contracts and transfer-pricing work; does not automatically shield liabilities | Consider for a real investment, jurisdictional or risk-separation need supported by professional advice |
| Separate technology/IP company and travel operating company | May distinguish platform licensing from regulated travel-marketplace relationships; can support distinct investors or licensing economics | Requires valid IP assignments/licence terms, real substance, related-party pricing, tax and transfer-pricing analysis; can complicate vendor contracts, employees and data roles | Consider only when business scale, external licensing, liability analysis or investors justify ongoing cost |
| Country subsidiaries as markets expand | Can support locally required contracting, employment, licensing, payments, tax or risk management | Duplicated compliance and accounting; cross-border group services and transfers require careful treatment | Consider market-by-market after local legal, commercial and tax due diligence |

These are business-structure options, not legal conclusions. Corporate separation alone does not guarantee liability protection, regulatory compliance, tax efficiency or investor readiness.

## 3. Evidence needed for OuterBlocks Limited and OuterBlocks Destinations Limited

Before production launch, gather and have the relevant professional verify:

- Current BRELA certificate of incorporation and company extract; current directors and shareholders; constitutional documents and permitted business objects.
- TIN and applicable TRA registration/tax status; accounting-period setup, tax filings, fiscal-receipt/e-invoicing applicability and tax-clearance requirements where relevant.
- Valid business licence for the activities, premises and relevant business class; confirm whether the exact travel distribution/air-ticketing/tourism activities trigger sector licences in addition to transport-system approvals.
- Required regulator permits/registrations based on the real operating role, including whether OuterBlocks is marketplace, software provider, ticketing-system owner, agent, merchant or another category.
- Bank and PSP merchant onboarding documents, beneficial-owner checks, settlement account ownership, refund liability and signatory/approval controls.
- Signed supplier/operator contracts with the contracting entity named consistently across API credentials, invoices, customer terms and settlements.
- Employment/contractor agreements and confidentiality/invention assignment clauses where lawful, with a complete IP chain for code, designs, domains, logos, data models and documentation.
- Privacy notices, processor agreements, vendor/subprocessor register, data retention plan, cross-border transfer assessments and applicable PDPC registrations/permits.
- Accounting policy for agent-versus-principal assessment, revenue recognition, commissions, taxes, FX, refund liabilities and intercompany balances if a group is ever created.

Tanzania's National Business Portal says business licensing generally requires a registered company/business name, suitable premises, TIN, any relevant sector permit and a valid business licence; it identifies BRELA as responsible for registration and explains that licence class/authority depends on the activity. The portal also identifies travel-agent/air-ticketing activities in the Tourist Agency Licence category. Travelar's exact role and licence classification must be confirmed rather than inferred from the brand name. Sources: https://www.business.go.tz/register-a-business and https://www.business.go.tz/obtain-a-business-license .

## 4. Intellectual property and product ownership

A clear IP chain matters regardless of the corporate structure:

1. Identify all contributors to software, brand assets, designs, documentation and other deliverables.
2. Ensure agreements establish ownership or enforceable rights for code and other work, including employee, founder and contractor contributions as applicable.
3. Inventory third-party components and open-source licences; maintain dependency/SBOM evidence and comply with licence conditions.
4. Record domain registrar ownership and authorized access. The domain `travelarway.com` and proposed subdomains must be verified in the relevant registrar/DNS accounts; public brand references do not prove legal ownership.
5. Store repositories, cloud accounts, signing keys and vendor master accounts under company-controlled access with recovery and offboarding procedures.
6. Avoid moving IP between entities without documented legal assignment/licensing, valuation where needed, and tax review.

Under the confirmed intended structure, determine which entity owns Travelar IP and which entity receives a written license or assignment. Document intercompany services, staff/contracting, cost allocation, tax and transfer-pricing treatment. Do not assume group licensing or cash movements are tax-neutral.

## 5. Commercial roles must match the legal entity

For every mode and provider, the contract and operating records must establish:

- Who supplies the travel service and who is the ticketing/reservation authority.
- Whether OuterBlocks acts as disclosed agent, marketplace intermediary, merchant of record, principal/reseller or technology provider.
- Who receives customer funds and bears refund/chargeback responsibility.
- Who issues invoices/fiscal receipts and recognizes which revenue.
- Who controls customer support, cancellations, changes, complaints, service disruption and supplier default.
- Which entity is controller/processor of each data flow and which subprocessors receive data.

A PSP account does not itself establish the permitted regulatory role. A GDS credential does not establish ticketing authority. A supplier API does not establish contractual rights to distribute every product. A separate subsidiary does not automatically eliminate parent or operating-company exposure.

## 6. Future holding company / subsidiaries: decision triggers

Do not add an entity solely to make the org chart appear investment-ready. Revisit only when one or more documented triggers exists:

- A real investor requires equity at a specific level or requests a clean group structure.
- A regulator or country-entry plan requires a locally incorporated entity.
- The business genuinely licenses technology to third parties outside the operating marketplace.
- Risk analysis identifies a specific liability/contracting separation whose benefit exceeds the administrative and tax costs.
- A different entity is needed for employees, assets, financing or strategic transactions.
- External shareholders need ring-fenced economics in a product line or geography.

Before any restructuring, produce a written decision with company setup/maintenance costs, taxes, transaction taxes, transfer pricing, contracts to novate, licences to reapply for, employee transfers, IP implications, banking/PSP changes, accounting consolidation and exit implications.

## 7. Investment-readiness checklist

### Corporate and governance

- Verified company records and approved constitutional objects.
- Current cap table and evidence for issued shares/beneficial ownership.
- Board/shareholder resolutions for major contracts, IP, debt, bank signatories and material risk decisions as applicable.
- Conflict-of-interest policy, delegated-authority matrix and record of approvals.
- No undocumented related-party balances or arrangements.

### Finance and controls

- Monthly management accounts; properly supported general ledger; revenue-recognition memo; tax filing calendar.
- Separate treatment for gross booking value, Travelar revenue, supplier payables, customer liabilities, PSP clearing and agent/cashier balances.
- Reconciliation of bookings, payments, supplier records and bank/PSP settlement.
- Refund/chargeback reserve policy and cash-flow scenarios.
- Audit-ready contracts, invoices and approval trail.

### IP, software and data

- IP assignment/ownership records; trademark/domain status checked by appropriate searches.
- Dependency/SBOM and open-source licence record; repository ownership and access controls.
- Security policies, incident-response plan, backup/restore test evidence, vulnerability management and vendor/subprocessor inventory.
- Privacy governance, lawful processing assessments, retention schedule and breach/transfer procedures.
- System architecture, API documentation, deployment records and product requirements traceability.

### Commercial diligence

- Executed supplier and operator agreements and verified production rights.
- Written commission, markup, refund, payout and service-level terms.
- Clear customer terms and complaint/refund route.
- Provider coverage map and dependence/outage plans.
- Measured customer/operator demand and transparent unit economics—not unsupported national market-size claims.

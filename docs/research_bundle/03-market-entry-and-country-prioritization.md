# 03 — Market Entry and Country Prioritization

**Product:** Travelar  
**Parent company:** OuterBlocks Limited
**Intended operating entity:** OuterBlocks Destinations Limited (formal verification required)  
**Version:** 0.2 — Stage 2 market research  
**Research cut-off:** 23 October 2026  
**Status:** Preliminary strategic assessment; not a completed country-by-country legal opinion

## 1. Executive conclusion

Travelar should validate Tanzania as its first market, with Kenya as the first major expansion market to investigate in depth after the Tanzania ticketing, supplier and settlement model is proven. This is a sequence recommendation, not a conclusion that either market is legally cleared. Uganda and Rwanda should remain in the next evaluation group; Ethiopia, Ghana, Nigeria and South Africa require separate market, regulatory and supplier due diligence before ranking them against one another.

A defensible market ranking cannot be based on population or tourist arrivals alone. Travelar requires a combination of lawful supplier access, actual bookable inventory, ticket-issuance authority, payment collection and settlement permissions, operator density, distribution economics, and the ability to support customers during service disruptions. Current public evidence does not support a credible national bus-market-size estimate for Tanzania: the National Bureau of Statistics' *Statistical Abstract 2025* expressly says reliable road-transport data remain scarce because the sector is fragmented and informal, and conventional surveys receive low or inconsistent responses. [NBS, Statistical Abstract 2025, Chapter 10](https://www.nbs.go.tz/uploads/statistics/documents/en-1790933026-Statistical%20Abstract%202025.pdf)

## 2. Evidence-led Tanzania market signals

### 2.1 Regulated online bus-ticketing is an established ecosystem

LATRA's 8 October 2026 update says 14 online-ticketing service providers have connected their systems to the Integrated Ticket Issuance System, CeTS, commonly known as Safari Tiketi. The list names Busbora Company Limited, Otapp Agency Company Limited, Abood Bus Service Limited, Duarani Innovate Limited, Shabiby Transporters, Hashtech Tanzania Limited, Iyishe Company Limited, Voltic Tanzania Limited, Web Corporation Tanzania Limited, Mkombozi Infotech Company Limited, Itule Company Limited, Sepa Tech Limited, Logix Company Limited and Global Light & Company Limited. The list mixes technology-company names and bus-operator names; it is not evidence that all 14 are independent retail marketplaces or that each has identical legal permissions. [LATRA, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.latra.go.tz/news/safari-tiketi-enhances-travel-assurance)

LATRA's 30 January 2026 notice identified 10 systems as having met requirements at that date and warned operators against use of unauthorized systems. The January count and October connected-provider count are time-specific regulatory statements and should not be silently reconciled as though they describe exactly the same status definition. Before launch, request a current list showing each legal entity, product/system name, certificate status, approved role and permitted production activities. [LATRA, 2 Feb 2026 publication](https://www.latra.go.tz/news/latra-gives-bus-operators-14-days-to-adopt-approved-e-ticketing-systems)

### 2.2 “Digital ticketing” is not the same as “digital payment adoption”

LATRA's October update reports that, across four years of the online-ticketing implementation, 62.18 million passengers used the systems; 59.27 million (95.3%) paid fares in cash and 2.91 million (4.7%) used online payment methods. The right interpretation is that digital systems can support ticket issuance and oversight while cash remains a dominant way to pay in the reported population. It does **not** prove that only 4.7% of all Tanzanian bus passengers use digital tickets, and it is not a national online-booking conversion rate. [LATRA, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.latra.go.tz/news/safari-tiketi-enhances-travel-assurance)

**Product implication:** Travelar should initially support an explicit multi-channel operating model: web/app bookings, operator counters, agent-assisted bookings, and permitted cash/electronic collection flows. Channel attribution, cash reconciliation, fiscal records, ticket validity and regulatory reporting must remain distinct. Do not design for app-only adoption.

### 2.3 Reliable national road-transport sizing is currently unavailable

NBS identifies fragmented road transport, small-scale operators, informality and inconsistent conventional survey returns as reasons road-transport figures are difficult to collect. This prevents a robust top-down estimate of long-distance bus total addressable market from the public statistics reviewed here. [NBS, Statistical Abstract 2025, p. 89 printed / Chapter 10](https://www.nbs.go.tz/uploads/statistics/documents/en-1790933026-Statistical%20Abstract%202025.pdf)

Other mode indicators demonstrate that transport demand spans multiple formal systems but should not be added into one “Travelar market size” without checking definitions and overlap. NBS reports 8.11 million air passengers in 2025 (3.77 million international and 4.34 million domestic), 3.047 million SGR passengers, 506,000 mainline passengers, and 2.080 million commuter passengers in its 2025 railway tables. Those data describe air and rail rather than the long-distance bus TAM, and passenger counts may not equal unique people or addressable third-party bookings. [NBS Statistical Abstract 2025, Tables 10.1–10.2](https://www.nbs.go.tz/uploads/statistics/documents/en-1790933026-Statistical%20Abstract%202025.pdf)

### 2.4 Demand and integration evidence to collect before sizing

Travelar should build a bottom-up model using, at minimum:

- Actual operator-level departures, seats/capacity, load factor and sold tickets by route and date.
- Ticket value and fare distributions by route/segment, class and season.
- Current ticketing platform and whether the operator owns, licenses or merely uses it.
- Ticket channel share (counter, phone/WhatsApp, agents, provider marketplace, operator site and other channels).
- Successful, failed, expired, cancelled and refunded booking counts.
- Share paid in cash, mobile money, cards or bank channels, separated from how the ticket was issued.
- Commission and payment fee borne by each party, as verified by contract rather than assumed.
- Cost of customer acquisition and customer-support burden.

Use at least 8–12 weeks of representative operational data from willing pilot operators, including peak and off-peak dates, and document sampling limitations. A short high-season sample should not be extrapolated as an annual run rate without adjustments.

## 3. Preliminary expansion order

The following order is a **research sequence**, not a completed attractiveness ranking. Regulatory feasibility and production supplier access are hard gates. No numerical country scores are assigned because comparable current evidence has not yet been assembled for all candidate countries.

| Stage | Market | Rationale for research priority | Evidence confidence | Gate before investment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Tanzania Mainland | Confirmed initial launch intent; an established LATRA/CeTS-connected long-distance bus ticketing ecosystem offers a concrete partner/authorization question. | High for existence of CeTS ecosystem; low for Travelar eligibility and unit economics. | Written LATRA classification and ticketing route; operator contracts; PSP/funds-flow review; registrations. |
| 0A | Zanzibar-linked ferry journeys | Strategic to a Tanzania-first multimodal experience, but maritime and cross-jurisdiction arrangements are distinct from Mainland bus ticketing. | Preliminary. | Verify operator, vessel/route, TASAC scope and Zanzibar requirements; obtain commercial inventory rights. |
| 1 | Kenya | Natural first expansion study because it is a neighboring market and has public intercity bus distribution through BuuPass plus official railway distribution through Kenya Railways. This also means competition and supplier contracts must be taken seriously. | High for visible consumer/distribution systems; incomplete for Travelar-specific access economics. | Separate Kenya legal opinion, ODPC/CBK/KRA/NTSA analysis as applicable, operator and rail agreements, local payment model. |
| 2 | Uganda and Rwanda (parallel due diligence) | East African regional connectivity creates a plausible corridor and product-reuse hypothesis. Public evidence gathered in this installment is insufficient to rank one above the other. | Low-to-moderate; research incomplete. | Comparable passenger/route data, legal classification, approved supplier pipeline, tax/data/payment maps. |
| 3 | Ethiopia, Ghana, Nigeria and South Africa (screening pool) | Candidate markets with potentially meaningful travel demand but different legal, payment, operating and competitive environments. Current work has not established a defensible ordering. | Low; not yet evaluated to comparable depth. | Country-specific legal, market, supplier, FX/settlement, payment and hosting due diligence. |

### Why Kenya is next for research, not automatically next for launch

BuuPass publicly advertises a bus marketplace with over 100 operators, 350+ routes, mobile-money/card/bank-payment options, and products including a Bus Management System. These are company-reported claims, not independently audited market statistics, but they show that a recognizable competitor is already in market. Kenya Railways also documents official online, counter and Safaricom USSD booking for Madaraka Express. A potential Travelar launch therefore requires actual differentiation and authorized inventory; a generic “one app for buses and trains” claim is insufficient. [BuuPass booking platform](https://buupass.com/booking); [BuuPass about](https://www.buupass.com/about-us); [Kenya Railways Madaraka Express FAQ](https://krc.co.ke/madaraka-express-faq/)

## 4. Recommended scoring framework for the next country study

After gathering comparable evidence, score each country from 1 (weak/high-friction) to 5 (strong/low-friction) and record evidence quality separately. Suggested weights total 100%:

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to use |
|---|---:|---|
| Legal/regulatory feasibility | 25% | Official statutes, written regulator responses, licences, data and payment requirements. A hard prohibition overrides the numeric score. |
| Production supplier/inventory access | 20% | Executed contracts, confirmed production booking rights and successful test/transaction evidence. |
| Bottom-up addressable demand | 15% | Operator-level seats, transactions and fares; credible transport statistics. |
| Contribution margin and liquidity | 15% | Contracted take-rate, PSP/supplier fees, refund and fraud losses, settlement lag and working capital. |
| Operator onboarding and systems interoperability | 10% | Operator interviews, system distribution, integration documentation and migration costs. |
| Consumer distribution and payment fit | 5% | Local payment reach, accessibility, language and assisted/offline channels. |
| Cross-border/corridor synergy | 5% | Origin/destination evidence, permitted cross-border services and shared suppliers. |
| Support, hosting and operating complexity | 5% | Language/time-zone support, data handling, infrastructure, incident-response and local staffing needs. |

Do not average away a hard legal blocker. A country can be commercially attractive but not launchable until the relevant authorization or supplier agreement exists.

## 5. Market hypotheses to test

1. **Hybrid distribution is essential.** Supported by LATRA's reported cash-heavy payment mix; validate exact channel shares by operator and route.
2. **OIMS can acquire suppliers even where the marketplace alone cannot.** Hypothesis: operators may value scheduling, counter sales, reconciliations and operational controls; validate willingness to pay and incumbent system compatibility through interviews and pilots.
3. **The best moat may be system interoperability and money correctness, not catalogue breadth.** Hypothesis: a dependable inventory/settlement layer could serve both Travelar-generated bookings and operator-owned channels, but demand and provider permission must be established.
4. **Multimodal consumer discovery should not precede bookable supply.** Hypothesis: a six-mode homepage may improve future brand positioning, but launch conversion depends on real inventory, confirmed fulfillment and clear support/refund rules.

These are hypotheses, not established market findings.

## 6. Required research actions

1. Request an anonymized route-level aggregate data extract or publicly available dashboard from LATRA; do not seek unnecessary passenger-level personal information.
2. Conduct structured interviews with at least 8 long-distance bus operators across different sizes and regions, 3 ticketing-system providers, 2 large terminal/branch sales teams, 2 PSPs and relevant regulator teams.
3. Test competitor booking flows on a small, repeatable sample of routes at weekday/off-peak and high-demand periods. Record timestamp, route, inventory shown, fare, booking journey, payment types, terms and final confirmation. Do not infer API access from a working website.
4. Obtain sample operator data and contracts under NDA where commercially appropriate; separate operator data from competitor screen observations.
5. Complete a Kenya regulatory and supplier study before assigning Kenya a scored launch attractiveness result.
